# Platform rules and screen flags: first purchase to first renewal

Checked 2026-10-30. Scope: the in-app screens between the first purchase (or trial start) and the first renewal: welcome after purchase, notification primer, reminder and habit setup, first-week guidance, streaks and progress, value recaps, in-app trial-ending notices, return screen after inactivity, rating prompt, widget prompts.

Text in quotes is copied from the linked page. Unquoted text is a close paraphrase. Each item is marked:
- **rule**: binding store or legal text;
- **guidance**: official but non-binding design guidance (HIG, Android developer docs, regulator papers);
- **case**: an enforcement action or decision;
- **pending**: not yet law.

"Secondary" means the text came through a law-firm or third-party page, not the official one. These are screen flags, not legal advice. The skill uses only items that a designer changes on a screen; anything that needs a lawyer or engineering is left out of the response.

## 1. Apple

### 1.1 App Review Guidelines ("Last Updated: October 16, 2026") — https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/

| ID | Guideline | Text | What it means on a screen | Tier |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | **4.5.4 Push Notifications** | "Push Notifications must not be required for the app to function, and should not be used to send sensitive personal or confidential information. Push Notifications should not be used for promotions or direct marketing purposes unless customers have explicitly opted in to receive them via consent language displayed in your app's UI, and you provide a method in your app for a user to opt out from receiving such messages." | (1) The notification primer can't block the app: no dead end if the user declines. (2) Allowing system push is **not** consent to marketing push. If reminders will also carry offers (win-back, discounts, upgrade nudges), the app needs its own consent wording in its UI and an in-app opt-out. (3) Our reading: habit or trial reminders that are not promotional don't need the extra consent, so keep the two kinds apart in the primer copy | rule |
| A2 | **4.5.3** | "Do not use Apple Services to spam, phish, or send unsolicited messages to customers, including Game Center, Push Notifications, Live Activities, etc." | Reminder cadence screens and "remind me" toggles must not lead to message volume the user didn't ask for | rule |
| A3 | **5.1.1(ii) Permission** | "Ensure your purpose strings clearly and completely describe your use of the data." "Paid functionality must not be dependent on or require a user to grant access to this data." | Purpose strings apply to protected data (camera, health, location…). Notification authorisation has no purpose string. **Our reading:** a paid feature (e.g. a premium "smart reminders" tier) shouldn't be locked behind a data permission the feature doesn't need | rule |
| A4 | **5.1.1(iv) Access** | "Apps must respect the user's permission settings and not attempt to manipulate, trick, or force people to consent to unnecessary data access." "Where possible, provide alternative solutions for users who don't grant consent." | After a declined permission, offer the fallback (in-app reminders list, a calendar export, an email reminder) instead of nagging. Apple's examples are data permissions; applying it to notification prompts is **our interpretation** | rule (applied by analogy) |
| A5 | **2.5.16** | "Widgets, extensions, and notifications should be related to the content and functionality of your app." | Widget and notification content must be about the app's own content (streak, progress, next lesson), not unrelated promotion | rule |
| A6 | **2.5.18** | "Display advertising should be limited to your main app binary, and should not be included in extensions, App Clips, widgets, notifications, keyboards, watchOS apps, etc." | No ads in widgets or notifications. Whether an upgrade prompt for the app's own plan counts as "display advertising" is not defined; treat promo content in widgets as a risk | rule |
| A7 | **3.2.2(x)** | "Apps must not force users to rate the app, review the app, download other apps, or other store-related actions in order to access functionality, content, or use of the app." | A rating ask can't gate anything: no "rate us to continue", no streak freeze or feature unlocked for a rating | rule |
| A8 | **5.6.1 App Store Reviews** | "Use the provided API to prompt users to review your app; this functionality allows customers to provide an App Store rating and review without the inconvenience of leaving your app, and we will disallow custom review prompts." | The rating ask is the system prompt (`requestReview`). A custom star-rating sheet that imitates it is disallowed | rule |
| A9 | Business section, introduction (Section 3) | "If we find that you have attempted to manipulate reviews, inflate your chart rankings with paid, incentivized, filtered, or fake feedback … we will take steps to preserve the integrity of the App Store, which may include expelling you from the Apple Developer Program." | **"Filtered" feedback is named.** A "Do you enjoy the app?" pre-screen that sends only happy users to the store prompt and unhappy users to a feedback form is a filtering risk. Apple doesn't spell out the pre-screen pattern; the link from "filtered" to this pattern is **our interpretation** | rule |
| A10 | **5.6 Developer Code of Conduct** (intro) | "Apps should never prey on users or attempt to rip off customers, trick them into making unwanted purchases, force them to share unnecessary data, raise prices in a tricky manner, charge for features or content that are not delivered, or engage in any other manipulative practices within or outside of the app." | Catch-all for manipulative streak, loss or guilt framing tied to purchases (e.g. "buy a streak repair or lose 200 days"), and for misleading trial-ending notices | rule |
| A11 | **5.6.3 Discovery Fraud** | "Manipulating any element of the App Store customer experience such as charts, search, reviews, or referrals to your app erodes customer trust and is not permitted." | Same as A9: no review manipulation | rule |

### 1.2 Asking for notification permission

| ID | Source | Text | What it means on a screen | Tier |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A13 | Apple developer docs, "Asking permission to use notifications" — https://developer.apple.com/documentation/usernotifications/asking-permission-to-use-notifications | "Make the request in a context that helps people understand why your app needs authorization. In a task-tracking app that sends reminder notifications, you might make the request after the person schedules a first task. Sending the request in context provides a better experience than automatically requesting authorization on first launch, because people can see the purpose your apps notifications serve." | Best moment: right after the user sets a reminder time, schedules a session or starts a streak. Not on the welcome-after-purchase screen by default | guidance |
| A14 | Same page, provisional authorisation | "Use provisional authorization to send notifications on a trial basis. People can then evaluate the notifications and decide whether to authorize them." "The system delivers provisional notifications quietly — they don't interrupt the person with a sound or banner, or appear on the lock screen. Instead, they only appear in the notification center's history. These notifications also include buttons that prompt the person to keep or turn off the notification." | An alternative to an up-front primer: no prompt shown. The trade-off is quiet delivery (no banner, no lock screen), which is weak for a time-critical trial-ending reminder. Flag a primer-less app as "provisional or none?" | guidance |
| A15 | HIG, Privacy → "Requesting permission" — https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/privacy | "Request permission only when your app clearly needs access to the data or resource." "Ideally, wait to request permission until people actually use an app feature that requires access." "Avoid requesting permission at launch unless the data or resource is required for your app to function." | Same as A13 for any permission asked in the first week (Health, camera, location) | guidance |
| A16 | HIG, Privacy → pre-alert screens | "If it's essential to provide additional details, you can display a custom screen or window before the system alert appears." "**Include only one button and make it clear that it opens the system alert.**" "Use a term like 'Continue' or 'Next' to title the single button in your custom screen or window." "**Don't include additional actions in your custom screen or window, unless needed to obtain a legal consent.** For example, don't provide a way for people to leave the screen or window without viewing the system alert — like offering an option to close or cancel." | Apple's pre-alert spec: one button ("Continue"/"Next"), no "Allow" label, no close or "Not now". **Scope caveat:** the HIG says these guidelines apply to custom views before alerts for "camera, microphone, location, contact, calendar, and tracking". Notifications are not in that list. A "Not now" on a notification primer is common and not explicitly banned; flag it only as a deviation from the HIG pattern | guidance |
| A17 | HIG, Privacy → prohibited designs (stated for tracking requests) | "Never precede the system-provided alert with a custom screen or window that could confuse or mislead people." Prohibited examples: offering incentives; "Don't display a custom screen that mirrors the functionality of the system alert. In particular, don't create a button title that uses 'Allow' or similar terms, because people don't allow anything in a pre-alert screen."; "Don't show an image of the standard alert and modify it in any way."; "Don't add a visual cue that draws people's attention to the system alert's Allow buttons." | Stated as rejection reasons for ATT pre-prompts. For notification primers they are best practice, not a stated rule: no fake system alert, no "Allow" button on the primer, no arrow pointing at "Allow", no reward for allowing | guidance (rule for ATT) |
| A18 | HIG, Managing notifications — https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/managing-notifications | "Don't use notifications to send marketing or promotional content unless people explicitly agree to receive such information." "Before you send these notifications to people, you must receive their explicit permission to do so. Create an alert, modal view, or other interface that describes the types of information you want to send and gives people a clear way to opt in or out." | The marketing-push consent from A1 is a designed screen or toggle that names the message types, with an equal opt-out. Check settings for a separate "offers and news" toggle | guidance (backs rule A1) |
| A19 | Same page, interruption levels | "Never use the Time Sensitive interruption level to send a marketing notification." "Use the Time Sensitive interruption level only for notifications that are relevant in the moment." "…make sure the notification is about an event that's happening now or will happen within an hour." | A trial-ending reminder sent days ahead is not Time Sensitive. A streak-about-to-break push at 11 pm arguably is "within an hour", but must not be marketing | guidance |
| A20 | HIG, Notifications — https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/notifications | "**Avoid sending multiple notifications for the same thing, even if someone hasn't responded.** … If you send multiple notifications for the same thing, you fill up Notification Center, and people may turn off all notifications from your app." "**Use a badge only to show people how many unread notifications they have.**" "**Avoid creating a custom image or component that mimics the appearance or behavior of a badge.**" | Reminder-frequency pickers and "we miss you" sequences: one reminder per event. In-app: don't fake red badges on tabs or the streak icon to pull users back | guidance |

|---|---|---|---|---|

| A24 | StoreKit docs, "Requesting App Store reviews" — https://developer.apple.com/documentation/storekit/requesting-app-store-reviews | "Make the request at a time that doesn't interrupt what someone is trying to achieve in your app, for example, at the end of a sequence of events that they successfully complete." ## 3. Regulators: dark patterns, nudges, streaks and notifications

Most of these are not app-store rules, and most subscription apps are not "online platforms" under the DSA. Use them to flag manipulative framing, not as a compliance verdict.

| ID | Source | What it says (only what the source states) | What it means on a screen | Tier |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | FTC staff report "Bringing Dark Patterns to Light" (September 2022) — https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/P214800%20Dark%20Patterns%20Report%209.14.2022%20-%20FINAL.pdf | Four categories: "Design Elements that Induce False Beliefs"; "…that Hide or Delay Disclosure of Material Information"; "…that Lead to Unauthorized Charges"; "…that Obscure or Subvert Privacy Choices". Appendix A defines "**Nagging**: Asking repeatedly and disruptively if a user wants to take an action"; p. 24 cites "repeatedly presenting the choices as 'Yes' or 'Not Now' instead of 'Yes' or 'No'". "**Confirmshaming**: Using shame to steer users away from certain choices" (example "No, I don't want to save money"). "**Baseless Countdown Timer**: showing a fake countdown clock that just goes away or resets when it times out". **The report does not mention streaks, gamification, loss aversion or "fear of missing out".** | Flags: a notification or rating re-ask on every launch (nagging); "No, I'll lose my progress" or "I don't care about my goals" as decline text (confirmshaming); a "streak ends in" or "offer ends in" timer that resets (baseless countdown). Staff report, not a rule | guidance |
| D2 | EU Digital Services Act, Art. 25 (Regulation (EU) 2022/2065). Text via https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/dsa/dsa-regulation/article-25/ (secondary transcription of OJ L 277; EUR-Lex was not reachable) | 25(1): "Providers of online platforms shall not design, organise or operate their online interfaces in a way that deceives or manipulates the recipients of their service or in a way that otherwise materially distorts or impairs the ability of the recipients of their service to make free and informed decisions." 25(2): does not apply to practices covered by the UCPD (2005/29/EC) or the GDPR. 25(3): the Commission may issue guidelines on "(a) giving more prominence to certain choices when asking the recipient of the service for a decision; (b) repeatedly requesting that the recipient of the service make a choice where that choice has already been made, especially by presenting pop-ups that interfere with the user experience; (c) making the procedure for terminating a service more difficult than subscribing to it." | **Scope:** binds "online platforms" (services that disseminate user content to the public). Still, 25(3)(b) is the clearest EU text against re-asking a declined notification or rating choice via pop-ups, and (a) against a primer whose "Allow" path is far more prominent than "Not now" | rule (narrow scope) |
| D3 | European Commission, Guidelines on the protection of minors under DSA Art. 28 (14 July 2025) — https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/library/commission-publishes-guidelines-protection-minors | Recommends "Disabling by default features that contribute to excessive use, like communication 'streaks,' ephemeral content, 'read receipts,' autoplay, or push notifications" and "removing persuasive design features aimed predominantly at engagement". | **The only official text found that names streaks.** It applies to online platforms accessible to minors. For a teen-facing or kids app: streaks and push off by default for minors. For adult apps it is a direction of travel, not a rule | guidance (DSA Art. 28 guidelines) |
| D4 | European Parliament resolution on addictive design of online services, 2023/2043(INI), adopted 12 Dec 2023 (545–12–61) — OEIL summary: https://oeil.europarl.europa.eu/oeil/en/document-summary?id=1769972 | Asks the Commission to "urgently assess the need to prohibit the most harmful practices not yet blacklisted", naming "infinite scrolling and default auto-play, constant push notifications or receive or read receipts". | D5 | EU Digital Fairness Act — https://www.europarl.europa.eu/legislative-train/theme-protecting-our-democracy-upholding-our-values/file-digital-fairness-act | **Not proposed yet.** Commission work programme 2026, expected Q4 2026; consultation closed 24 Oct 2025. Expected to tackle "the addictive design of digital products" and "deceptive or addictive interface design". Streaks and notifications are not named on the page | Watch item. Don't cite as law | pending |
| D6 | ICO and CMA joint position paper "Harmful design in digital markets" — https://www.drcf.org.uk/siteassets/drcf/pdf-files/harmful-design-in-digital-markets-ico-cma-joint-position-paper.pdf (the opened text shows no date; it references the DRCF 2023-24 workplan) | Practices: "harmful nudges and sludge", "confirmshaming" ("pressuring or shaming someone into doing something by making them feel guilty or embarrassed for not doing it"), "biased framing" (stressing the benefits of one option or the risks of the other), "bundled consent", "default settings". ## 4. Where the platforms disagree

| Topic | Apple | Google | Practical flag |
|---|---|---|---|
| Decline on a permission primer | HIG pre-alert pattern: one "Continue/Next" button, no close or cancel (A16; stated for data permissions and tracking, not notifications) | Rationale UI must offer "cancel" / "no thanks" (G4) | On iOS a primer without "Not now" is HIG-consistent; on Android it breaks guidance. A cross-platform primer with both a "Continue" and a "Not now" is acceptable on Android and not banned on iOS for notifications |
| When a primer is needed | Optional; provisional authorisation can skip the ask (A14) | Officially only after a first denial (G3) | A primer before the first ask is a design choice, not a requirement on either platform |
| Rating prompt cap | 3 per 365 days (A23, A24) | Undisclosed quota (G9) | Never design a flow that depends on the prompt showing |
| Pre-question before the rating ask | "Filtered" feedback named as manipulation (A9, interpretation); custom prompts disallowed (A8) | Explicitly banned: no "Do you like the app?" before or while showing the card (G7) | Flag any sentiment gate before the system rating prompt |

## 5. Screen flags for the skill

❌ = rule or explicit guidance; ⚠️ = guidance or our interpretation.

1. ❌ The app works if notifications are declined: no blocking primer and no dead end (A1, G4).
2. ❌ Marketing or offer push needs its own in-app consent wording and an in-app opt-out, separate from the system permission (A1, A18). Reminders and offers on one toggle = bundled consent (D6).
3. ⚠️ Ask in context: after the user sets a reminder, schedules something or starts a streak, not on first launch or straight after purchase (A13, A15, G2).
4. ⚠️ iOS primer: one "Continue"/"Next" button, no "Allow" label, no fake system alert, no arrow to "Allow", no reward for allowing (A16, A17). Android rationale: include "No thanks" (G4).
5. ⚠️ Save the asks: Android stops showing the system dialog after two denials (G5). Re-asks after that go to Settings and need a fresh, specific reason (D2 25(3)(b) on repeated requests).
6. ⚠️ After a decline, offer a fallback (in-app reminder list, calendar or email) and say what won't work (A4, G4).
7. ⚠️ One reminder per event. No repeated pushes for the same thing, no fake badges (A20). No marketing as Time Sensitive (A19). No "constant" push by default (D4).
8. ❌ Rating: system prompt only (A8, G8). No rating gate on any feature (A7). No reward for rating (A7, G10). ❌ on Google / ⚠️ on Apple: no "Enjoying the app?" pre-screen (G7, A9).
9. ⚠️ Rating timing: after a completed task, not in onboarding, not on the welcome-after-purchase screen, not at launch, not from a button (A21, A22, A24, G6, G9). A "Rate us" button deep-links to the store.
10. ⚠️ Streaks and loss framing: no confirmshaming on declines or lapses (D1, D6). No resetting "streak ends in" timer (D1). No buying a streak back under pressure (A10, our interpretation). Streaks off by default for minors (D3).
11. ⚠️ Widgets show live personal content and deep-link to it, not a logo tile (A25). ❌ No ads in widgets or notifications (A6). Live Activities only for bounded tasks of up to 8 hours, never promotions (A27).

## 6. Not verified or not found
