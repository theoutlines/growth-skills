# Web2app funnels and web checkout: Mobbin flows

Research for a skill that tears down web2app funnels and web checkout for mobile subscription apps. Collected 2026-10-30 with the Mobbin MCP (`search_flows`, `search_screens`). Mostly web, plus iOS for in-app link-outs and app login.

Rules followed:
- Every screen is described in my own words. Button labels are quoted only as short fragments.
- No images were downloaded or stored. Each row links its `mobbin_url`.
- Prices are copied as Mobbin captured them, with the capture's region and date. They are observed, not benchmarks.
- Evidence tier for everything here is **observed** (seen in a top app, no public test).

Caveats that apply to the whole file:
- **Mobbin web captures are desktop-width.** Mobile-web layouts aren't shown. That matters because most web2app traffic comes from paid social on phones. Wallet buttons like Apple Pay also depend on the browser and device, so a missing Apple Pay button on a desktop capture doesn't mean the app lacks one.
- **The classic paid-social quiz funnels are not on Mobbin** (Noom, BetterMe, Simple, Headway, Flo web, Mimo web, Fastic, Lasta and similar). Searches for them returned unrelated apps. The quiz-funnel rows below come from Calm, Headspace, Canva, ChatGPT, Perplexity, Rocket Money and Hims/Hers, whose web onboarding has the same structure. See Gaps.
- Buckets are RevenueCat SOSA buckets, taken from the app's primary App Store category. A bucket marked "(verify)" is my best guess. "AI" is a tag, not a bucket.

## 2. Patterns seen (own words, counts, examples)

Counts are apps, not screens. The denominator varies by pattern, so each line says what it counts across.

1. **"$0 due today" with a dated future charge (12 apps).** The checkout or paywall states nothing is charged today and names the date or the day number when the full price starts. Examples: Headspace, Calm, Duolingo, Canva, Spotify, Codecademy, Open, Whop, YouTube Premium, Uber One, Rocket Money, Givingli. A named calendar date (Canva, Spotify, Codecademy, Whop, Uber One, Rocket Money, Babbel's renewal date) is clearer than "after 7 days".

2. **PayPal next to card (12 of 17 apps with a visible payment step); Apple Pay rarely shown (3).** PayPal appears in Headspace, Calm, Spotify, Babbel, Grammarly, Canva, Codecademy, Paramount+, HBO Max, ExpressVPN, YouTube and Zendesk. Apple Pay is shown first only in Headspace, Uxcel and Whop, each with an "or" divider before the card. Duolingo showed card only. Caveat: these are desktop captures, and wallet buttons depend on the browser.

3. **Auto-renew disclosure written out next to the buy button (13 apps).** A sentence near the CTA gives the renewal price, the cadence, how to cancel, and sometimes refund rules. Examples: Paramount+ (inside a checkbox), ExpressVPN, Grammarly, Duolingo, Babbel, Uxcel, Whop, YouTube, Codecademy, Uber One, Rocket Money, Givingli, Canva. Most use consent-by-continuing text. Separate unchecked checkboxes appear in Headspace, Zendesk and Hims; Paramount+ is ambiguous.

4. **Goal/intent quiz before the paywall (9 apps).** One to seven short questions with single- or multi-select options, usually with a progress bar. Examples: Headspace, Calm (the first question is in the hero), Canva, ChatGPT, Perplexity, Hims, Hers, Rocket Money, Clay (B2B, not counted in the table). A "Skip" escape is offered by Headspace, Canva and ChatGPT; a back control by Calm, Canva, Hers and Hims. Only Hers and Hims turn the answers into a personalised result (score or "treatment match"). No mental-wellness or edtech funnel on Mobbin shows a "your plan is ready" reveal.

5. **Account before payment (9) vs after payment (1).** Most web checkouts here make an account first: Headspace (the form is on the paywall page), Calm ("save your preferences"), Open, HBO Max, Hulu (email-first), Canva (email code), ChatGPT, Perplexity, Rocket Money. Only ExpressVPN takes just an email at checkout and asks for a password after payment, offering a generated one. Whop also asks only for an email. This is a key difference from paid-social web2app funnels, which usually take email → pay → set up the account (see Gaps).

6. **Trial timeline and reminder promise (6 apps).** A vertical three-step strip (today / reminder / charge), or a sentence promising a reminder N days before the charge. Visual timelines: Headspace (reminder day 5 of 7), Duolingo (day 12 of 14, or 5 of 7), Canva (7 days before a 30-day trial ends), Spotify (7 days before). Sentence only: Calm (2 days before). Hims uses the same timeline shape for the prescription process. Duolingo also gives the reminder its own pre-checkout screen.

7. **Discount framing: strike-through, "save X%", per-month equivalent (12 apps).** Babbel (Save 41%, struck reference total), Uxcel, Higgsfield, Base44, Whop, Givingli (save 50%), HBO Max (save $/month chip), Paramount+ (annual saves ≥16%), ElevenLabs (2 months free), Uber One (save 20% annual), Headspace win-back, Amazon/Kindle (−50%). Per-month equivalents for annual plans: Calm, Duolingo, Babbel, Grammarly, Uxcel, Higgsfield, Uber One. Babbel is the only app that footnotes how its reference price is calculated.

8. **Live countdown timers (4 apps with a ticking clock, 2 more with "ends soon" text).** Uxcel (HH:MM:SS banner, repeated at checkout), Higgsfield ("one-time offer", AI), Base44 (46-hour "welcome offer", AI), Codecademy (dd:hh:mm:ss sale banner). Text-only urgency: Whop ("offer ends soon"), Calm ("Limited Time Offer" pill), Spotify iOS (dated end of offer). Timers cluster in AI-creative and edtech apps. None of the wellness apps used one.

9. **Annual nudge at the moment of choice (6 apps).** Paramount+ (switch off by default), Uxcel (switch on by default, a pre-set choice), ElevenLabs (modal with equal-weight buttons when monthly is picked), Headspace (annual/monthly tabs on the paywall), Uber One (annual "best value"), Zendesk (B2B, switch off).

10. **Post-purchase upsell or cross-sell (6 consumer apps).** Grammarly (add seats, with "Later"), Surfshark (extra 12 months at a one-time price, with "Skip deal"), Hims (25% off another category for 30 days), Babbel (refer a friend), Substack (preselected free subscriptions), YouTube (benefits reveal). True one-click order bumps on the checkout page itself did not appear in any subscription app. The only one-click post-purchase offer with a timer was an e-commerce flow (Shop, not in the table).

11. **"Get the app" handoff after the web purchase (3 subscription apps).** HBO Max (QR code plus store badges on the thank-you page), Rocket Money (an SMS download link sent automatically, with a "try a different number" fallback), Spotify (a persistent "Install App" button in the web player). All the others land the buyer in a web product (Headspace, Calm, Duolingo, Canva, Grammarly, ChatGPT, Perplexity, Paramount+), so there is no app handoff at all.

12. **Passwordless sign-in (4 apps).** Email one-time code: Canva (web sign-up). Email-first, create-if-new: Hulu (shared parent-company identity). Magic links in iOS apps: Cal AI (AI) and Hatch Sleep, which also offers a password fallback. This is the mechanism a web-bought account needs for its first app login. No Mobbin flow shows the full chain (web purchase → email → deep link → app opens signed in).

13. **Trust and risk reversal at checkout (8 apps).** Money-back guarantees: ExpressVPN (30 days), Babbel (20 days). SSL or "secure payment" notes: Uxcel, Babbel, ExpressVPN. "Bank-level encryption" chip: Rocket Money. Testimonial and customer logos: Canva. Award logo: ExpressVPN. reCAPTCHA: Headspace, Spotify, Canva, Codecademy.

14. **Payment authentication and failure states (5 apps).** A 3-D Secure heads-up before submit (Zendesk). CVC re-entry for a saved card (YouTube). A disclosed small charge-and-refund to authorise the card (Canva). Inline "payment could not be processed" or "card declined" messages (Paramount+, Zendesk). No capture shows a bank's 3-D Secure challenge window itself.

15. **iOS link-out to web checkout, US (2 clear, 2 partial).** Spotify: the primary CTA has an external-link arrow, and small print right under it says the user will be taken to the website, followed by the offer terms. Amazon Shopping/Kindle: "Get book" with an arrow plus a helper line that purchase options are on the website. Fable: outside retailers listed above the in-app purchase (affiliate framing). Patreon: "Join" with no visible external indicator, so the mechanism is unconfirmed. None of the captures show Apple's system "leaving the app" sheet before the browser opens.

16. **Preselection to watch for (4 apps).** Uxcel: yearly toggle preset on. Substack: recommended subscriptions all preselected. Rocket Money: bank account checkbox preselected (it's also the only option). Paramount+: auto-renew consent box shown ticked, origin unclear. The opposite, explicitly unchecked consent, appears in Headspace (terms), Hims (terms and telehealth), YouTube ("remember card") and Zendesk.

### The 8 most common, ranked by count
1. Auto-renew disclosure by the CTA: 13
2. "$0 today" plus a dated future charge: 12
3. PayPal next to card: 12 (Apple Pay shown in only 3)
4. Discount framing (strike-through, save %, monthly equivalent): 12
5. Goal quiz before the paywall: 9
6. Account created before payment: 9 (after payment: 1)
7. Trust and risk-reversal elements: 8
8. Trial timeline or reminder promise: 6; tied with annual nudge (6) and post-purchase upsell (6)

## 3. Category notes per bucket

**Health & Fitness** (Headspace, Calm, Open, Hims, Hers, Cal AI, Hatch Sleep)
- These have the most complete trial-to-paid scaffolding: quiz → account → paywall with a value checklist → $0-today checkout → a timeline or reminder promise. Calm and Headspace both promise a reminder before the charge.
- No countdown timers. The one urgency cue is Calm's "Limited Time Offer" pill.
- Hims/Hers are the only funnels that turn answers into a result (a score, a match). They also defer the charge until a provider approves, a model specific to telehealth.
- Headspace uses a struck-through annual price for win-back, not at first purchase.
- Login for web buyers: Cal AI (AI) and Hatch use email magic links.

**Education** (Duolingo, Babbel, Uxcel, Codecademy)
- Two styles. Duolingo is trial-led: a calm trial timeline, a whole screen for the reminder promise, card only. Uxcel and Codecademy are discount-led: countdown banners and a preset yearly toggle (Uxcel).
- Babbel stands out for a lifetime plan next to the annual one, a footnoted reference price, a money-back guarantee, and a full renewal summary with dates and VAT.
- Codecademy shows BNPL (Afterpay) but disables it for monthly plans.

**Productivity, AI-tagged** (ChatGPT, Grammarly, Perplexity, Base44, ElevenLabs)
- The paywall is a tier comparison table, not a single offer. ChatGPT has four tiers, Grammarly two, Base44 four.
- Mostly no trial at web checkout (Grammarly charges $30 today). Discounts are segment-based (Perplexity school 50%) or time-boxed (Base44 welcome-offer timer).
- Light personalisation questions with Skip (ChatGPT, Perplexity). They feed recommendations, not a plan reveal.
- Post-purchase pushes seat expansion (Grammarly).

**Photo & Video** (Canva, YouTube, Higgsfield)
- Canva runs the cleanest combined paywall and checkout in the set: a 30-day trial, a dated timeline, a Skip escape, and a disclosed card-authorisation charge.
- Higgsfield (AI) is the most aggressive pricing page: a "personal" discount, a one-time-offer clock and stacked strike-throughs.
- YouTube re-verifies the CVC on saved cards before the trial starts.

**Media & Entertainment** (Spotify, Paramount+, HBO Max, Hulu)
- Checkouts ask for an address for tax (Spotify, Paramount+). They carry long auto-renew consent text, and Paramount+ puts it in a checkbox.
- Bundles and partner-service upsells dominate the landing pages (HBO Max, Hulu).
- Spotify is the main US iOS link-out example. HBO Max's thank-you page is the clearest "download the app" handoff (QR plus badges).

**Utilities** (ExpressVPN, Surfshark)
- Email-only checkout with the account set up after payment (ExpressVPN, generated password). Very wide payment-method coverage (crypto and local methods). Strong risk reversal (30-day money-back).
- Post-purchase "extra months" upsell (Surfshark), with a skip button the same size as the accept button.

**Social & Lifestyle** (Givingli, Substack, Patreon, Fable)
- Givingli: a standard two-plan modal with a "save 50%" annual and a 1-week trial.
- Substack's post-purchase step preselects everything.
- Patreon and Fable show in-app join or buy actions. Only Fable visibly sends users to outside retailers.

**Finance (verify bucket)** (Rocket Money)
- The only funnel with a stepped header that names "Download the app" as a funnel step. It also uses pay-what-you-think-is-fair framing and bank-account debit as the payment method.

**Food & Drink, Shopping, Business** (Uber One, Amazon/Kindle, Whop, Zendesk): references only. Uber One shows a strong single-modal trial disclosure. Kindle is a US link-out. Whop is a creator checkout with Apple Pay first plus Cash App. Zendesk shows the 3-D Secure notice.

## 4. Gaps

1. **The paid-social quiz funnels themselves are missing.** Noom, BetterMe, Simple, Headway, Flo, Mimo, Fastic, Lasta, Liven and the astrology and AI-companion funnels returned no matching flows on Mobbin. The core web2app patterns therefore have no Mobbin evidence:
   - long quizzes (20–40 steps)
   - fake "analysing" loaders and "your plan is ready" reveals
   - before/after projection charts
   - email capture before the paywall
   - pre-paywall "discount unlocked" scratch cards or spinners
   - weekly or 4-week plans with a struck-through intro price and a per-day price
   - checkout-page order bumps and post-purchase one-click upsell chains
   
   These need another public source (funnel teardown blogs, FunnelFox/Adapty/RevenueCat public galleries, or recordings) or our own walkthroughs described in our own words.
2. **No per-day pricing** ("$0.xx/day") on any web paywall captured here. The per-month equivalent is as granular as it gets.
3. **No mobile-web captures.** All web flows are desktop-width. Thumb-zone CTA placement, sticky price bars and wallet-first layouts on phones can't be checked from Mobbin.
4. **Apple Pay and Google Pay coverage is unreliable.** Wallet buttons depend on the browser. Google Pay appears in none of the web checkouts.
5. **No 3-D Secure challenge screen.** Only the pre-warning (Zendesk) and CVC re-verify (YouTube) are visible.
6. **No complete web → app login chain.** No flow shows: web purchase → confirmation email → magic link or deep link → app opens already signed in with the entitlement. The pieces exist separately (HBO Max thank-you page, Rocket Money SMS link, Cal AI and Hatch magic links) but never as one app's chain.
7. **No Apple External Purchase or link-entitlement system sheet** in any subscription app capture. US link-out evidence is limited to Spotify and Amazon/Kindle button styling and copy.
8. **Few Android link-out or alternative-billing examples.** None were searched, and none surfaced.
9. **Gaming, Travel and Business buckets** have no consumer web2app examples here. Social & Lifestyle is thin (dating apps' web checkouts not found).
10. **Region and price drift.** Some captures are from non-US storefronts (YouTube CA, Calm US$, Babbel shows VAT, Whop with Indonesia billing). Prices are as captured and may be out of date. Use them only as illustrations.
11. **Order-bump evidence is e-commerce only** (Shop app post-purchase offer with a 1:48 timer), so it was left out of the table. Don't cite it as a subscription-app pattern.
